## 2. Status today: test phase, then a closed pilot

Field Companion is not yet available to customers.

**Today: test phase.** Only people we invite can test Field Companion, in test organisations that we run ourselves. It runs on a test set-up, with a WhatsApp test number that works with a small number of registered phones. Testers must use **sample data**: made-up sites, visits, photos and reports. They must not record real occupants, owners, tenants, claimants, real addresses or real cases.

**Real personal data in the test phase.** Some data is real even now:

- the names, WhatsApp numbers and WhatsApp profile names of the people who test Field Companion;
- their own messages, voice notes, what they say during a walkthrough, and the photos they take (their voice and words are real, even when the visit is made up);
- messages from people who write to the Field Companion number, and from people who contact us;
- data about visitors to our website.

For testers, we are the controller (see section 6). During the test phase, AI requests go through a provider's global service, which may process them outside the EU and without zero data retention. Sections 6, 8, 10 and 11 explain who receives this data today and where.

**Next: closed pilot.** After the test phase, organisations that we invite can use Field Companion in a **closed pilot**. The closed pilot runs only on our live set-up, and starts only when all of the following are in place:

- a signed data processing agreement between us and each provider that handles personal data for Field Companion, with a safeguard for every transfer outside the EU;
- AI language models called through an **EU endpoint with zero data retention** on every request;
- backups, with an encrypted second copy at a separate provider in Germany, and central logs;
- this notice, published at the address the product links to.

In the closed pilot, organisations first use sample data. The names, numbers, messages and voices of their members are still real.

**Real customer data** (real sites, visits and people at sites) is allowed only after we confirm it in writing to the organisation, and only after the organisation and we have signed a full data processing agreement.

Where this notice says **"from launch"**, it means from the start of the closed pilot.

## 3. Two roles: your organisation and us

**Your organisation is the controller for its content.** An organisation (for example, a valuation firm, an engineering firm or a facilities team) uses Field Companion for its site visits. It decides who may use it, what is recorded, and how long it is kept. It is responsible for the data its people and its visits put into Field Companion. It must tell its own people and the people at the sites it inspects about this. We call this data "customer content".

**We are the processor for customer content.** We process customer content only on the organisation's documented instructions, under the data processing terms in our customer terms (Article 28 GDPR). Before real customer data, we and the organisation sign a full data processing agreement. We do not use customer content for our own purposes. If we ever wanted to, we would first need a legal basis and would tell you before we start.

**We are the controller for a small set of our own data:**

- data of people who test Field Companion for us in our own test organisations;
- messages from WhatsApp numbers that belong to no organisation;
- security and audit records;
- records of what the service used and cost (usage metering);
- contracts, account contacts, the list of numbers allowed to create an organisation, and proof that terms were accepted;
- invoices and accounting;
- privacy requests, complaints and records of data breaches;
- visitors to the Field Companion pages of our website, and business contacts and pilot enquiries.

## 8. About the AI

**What it does.** Field Companion uses AI models to understand what you say and write, turn speech into text and text into speech, file notes under the right area, match photos to the checklist, describe photos, answer questions, and draft the report.

**It tells you it is an AI.** Field Companion says it is an AI assistant, not a person: in the first message after you join, in its one reply to an uninvited number, in the first message after you agree to help a colleague, and at the start of every walkthrough (spoken and on screen). Every visit report states that it was drafted by AI and reviewed by a named person, and the PDF file carries a marker that it was drafted by AI.

**It can be wrong.** It may mishear, file a note under the wrong area, match the wrong photo, or add a detail that is not true. That is why uncertain matches are shown as suggestions, and why **a person reviews and submits every visit report** and is responsible for its content. Check the report before you submit it.

**Period reports** (summaries over a period, worded by AI from figures calculated by the software) are marked "not reviewed by a person" and are sent only to the member who asked for them.

**No automated decisions about you.** Field Companion does not make decisions about you that have legal effects or similarly significant effects (Article 22 GDPR). It does not score or rate the people who use it, it does not measure their work, and it does not recognise emotions or identify people. A report can say whether the visit was made on site and who reviewed it; it does not judge the person. Our customer terms do not allow organisations to use it for such purposes.

**No training on customer content.** We do not use customer content to train or fine-tune AI models. From launch, the AI language models are called through an EU endpoint with **zero data retention** on every request, so the model providers do not keep the content of requests or learn from it. The speech service is told on every request not to use audio to improve its models.

**During the test phase**, only sample data and the testers' own messages, voice, transcripts and photos are sent to the models. These requests go through OpenRouter's global endpoint, under its standard settings, and the language models are Anthropic's, used through our own Anthropic account (United States). Each provider keeps request data under its own terms.

**Records of AI requests.** To find faults, we keep records of requests to the AI language models, with an EU company (Langfuse, Germany). These records can include messages, what was said and photo descriptions. Today they hold testers' data and sample data only. We use these records only in the test phase. They are not deleted automatically today; we delete them when the test phase ends. From launch, we do not keep these records. If we ever start keeping them again, we will first set them to be deleted after 30 days and update this notice and our subprocessor list.

## 13. Security

What is in place today:

- Connections to Field Companion are encrypted (TLS 1.2 or higher). Live audio is encrypted in transit.
- The database, files and storage are encrypted at rest by the hosting provider.
- Each organisation's data is kept apart by database rules (row-level security) on every table. The organisation is always taken from the verified sign-in, never from a request or from the AI.
- Sign-in access tokens are valid for 15 minutes. Files are private. Links to download them work for 5 to 10 minutes, and links to upload them for up to 2 hours, after access is checked. Walkthrough links work once, on one phone, expire and can be withdrawn.
- We do not look at customer content, except at the organisation's request or for an incident, and we record when we do.
- Walkthrough audio is not stored. The Field Companion web page uses no cookies and loads no third-party scripts.

Before the closed pilot starts, we add: hardware security keys for all admin accounts, backups with an encrypted second copy in Germany, and central logs with alerts.

Field Companion has **not yet been audited or certified**, and no independent penetration test has been done yet.

If a data breach affects customer content, we tell the organisation without undue delay. For our own data, we report a breach to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens within 72 hours, unless it is unlikely to put anyone at risk. We tell you directly if it is likely to put you at high risk.
